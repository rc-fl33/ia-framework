## Purpose
This policy establishes comprehensive requirements for protecting data privacy across all artificial intelligence systems operated by {{ORGANIZATION_NAME}}. It addresses the unique data handling challenges posed by AI technologies, including model training data governance, inference data processing, output data ownership, and the prevention of unauthorized data exposure through AI-generated outputs. This policy implements the data and privacy controls defined by AIUC-1 Principle A (controls A001 through A007) and aligns with applicable data protection regulations including GDPR, CCPA, and sector-specific privacy requirements.

## Scope
This policy applies to all AI systems, platforms, and services that process, store, generate, or transmit data on behalf of {{ORGANIZATION_NAME}}, its employees, customers, partners, and third-party service providers. Coverage includes foundation model interactions, fine-tuned models, AI agent workflows, embedding generation, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines, and any AI-enabled feature that handles organizational or customer data. The policy governs data throughout its lifecycle from collection through deletion across all deployment environments including cloud, on-premises, and hybrid configurations.

## Definitions

**Input Data**: Any data provided to an AI system for processing, including user prompts, context documents, uploaded files, database queries, and system instructions.

**Output Data**: Any data generated by an AI system in response to input data, including text responses, generated code, images, recommendations, tool call results, and structured data outputs.

**Training Data**: Datasets used to train, fine-tune, or adapt AI models, including labeled examples, reinforcement learning feedback, and preference data.

**Inference Data**: Data processed during real-time AI system operation that is not used for model training, including session context, temporary processing data, and response generation inputs.

**Data Subject**: An identified or identifiable natural person whose personal data is processed by an AI system.

**Cross-Customer Data Exposure**: Unauthorized leakage of one customer's data to another customer through shared AI model contexts, training data contamination, or output generation.

**Data Sovereignty**: The principle that data is subject to the laws and governance structures of the jurisdiction in which it is collected or processed.

## Policy Requirements

### 1. Input Data Management (AIUC-1: A001)

**1.1 Input Data Usage Policies**
- {{ORGANIZATION_NAME}} shall establish and communicate clear policies governing how customer input data is used for model training, inference processing, and system improvement
- All input data usage policies shall be communicated through accessible terms of service, privacy notices, and in-product disclosures
- Policies shall specify whether input data is used for model training, and if so, under what conditions and with what safeguards
- Documentation shall justify retention periods for each data category with reference to business necessity and regulatory requirements

**1.2 Opt-In and Opt-Out Mechanisms**
- Customers shall be provided clear opt-in or opt-out mechanisms for input data usage in model training
- Opt-out requests shall be processed within defined timeframes and confirmed to the requesting party
- The default data handling posture (opt-in vs. opt-out) shall be documented and communicated prior to data collection
- Mechanisms shall be technically verified to ensure opted-out data is excluded from training pipelines

**1.3 Data Subject Rights**
- Procedures shall be established for customer data subject rights including access, portability, correction, and deletion
- Data subject access requests shall be fulfilled within timeframes required by applicable regulations
- AI-specific data subject rights considerations shall be addressed, including the right to know whether personal data was used in model training
- Deletion requests shall encompass both raw data stores and any derived representations where technically feasible

### 2. Output Data Ownership and Usage (AIUC-1: A002)

**2.1 Output Ownership Rights**
- Clear ownership rights shall be defined distinguishing between customer input contributions and AI-generated output content
- Output ownership policies shall address intellectual property considerations for AI-generated content
- Terms of service shall specify whether {{ORGANIZATION_NAME}} retains any rights to customer-prompted outputs
- Ownership determinations shall account for jurisdictional variations in AI output intellectual property law

**2.2 Output Usage and Consent**
- Consent and opt-out procedures shall be disclosed for the use of AI outputs in model improvement, analytics, or other secondary purposes
- Customers shall be informed if their outputs may be used to train or improve AI models
- Output usage policies shall be communicated through accessible and understandable terms of service
- Secondary use of outputs shall require affirmative consent where required by applicable regulations

**2.3 Output Deletion**
- Customers shall have the ability to request deletion of their AI-generated outputs from {{ORGANIZATION_NAME}} systems
- Output deletion procedures shall specify scope, timeframes, and confirmation mechanisms
- Backup and archival systems shall be included in deletion scope where technically feasible

### 3. Data Access Controls (AIUC-1: A003)

**3.1 Task-Relevant Data Access**
- AI agent data access shall be restricted to task-relevant information based on user roles and operational context
- Data collection limits shall be configured to enforce time-bounded, task-specific, purpose-limited access to contextual data
- Scoping actions shall be implemented based on agent objectives, session type, or workflow stage to minimize data exposure

**3.2 Role-Based Access Integration**
- AI system data access shall align with organizational identity and access management (IAM) policies
- Agent permission matrices shall be maintained mapping AI system capabilities to authorized data sources
- Dynamic context-based restrictions shall adjust data access when user roles, environments, or security contexts change
- Principle of least privilege shall be enforced for all AI agent data access operations

**3.3 Session Isolation**
- User sessions shall be isolated to prevent cross-session data leakage within AI systems
- Session data shall be purged according to defined retention schedules
- Multi-tenant environments shall enforce logical or physical data separation between customer sessions

### 4. Credential and IP Protection (AIUC-1: A004)

**4.1 Foundation Model Provider Safeguards**
- Foundation model provider safeguards for IP protection shall be documented and assessed for adequacy
- Provider commitments regarding data isolation, non-use of customer data for training, and confidentiality shall be verified
- Provider security certifications and compliance attestations shall be maintained on file

**4.2 Supplementary Controls**
- Supplementary data access controls shall be established where provider protections are insufficient for {{ORGANIZATION_NAME}} risk tolerance
- Input preprocessing shall detect and redact sensitive information before transmission to AI model providers
- Output monitoring procedures shall be implemented with automated review for high-risk scenarios where IP leakage is possible

**4.3 Intellectual Property Safeguards**
- AI systems shall be configured to prevent leakage of company trade secrets, proprietary algorithms, confidential business strategies, and competitive intelligence
- Sensitive document classification systems shall be integrated with AI data pipelines to prevent unauthorized processing of restricted content
- Regular testing shall be conducted to verify that IP protection controls function as intended

### 5. Cross-Customer Data Isolation (AIUC-1: A005)

**5.1 Data Isolation Controls**
- Customer data isolation controls shall be implemented with logical or physical separation to prevent cross-customer data exposure
- Explicit consent shall be obtained and disclosed when customer data may be combined with data from other customers or competitors
- Multi-tenant AI deployments shall implement tenant-aware routing to prevent data blending across customer boundaries

**5.2 Privacy-Enhancing Technologies**
- Privacy-enhancing technologies such as differential privacy, federated learning, or secure enclaves shall be evaluated and deployed where appropriate
- Inference-time isolation mechanisms shall prevent one customer's data from influencing another customer's outputs
- Data combination points shall be identified and verified for isolation control effectiveness

**5.3 Verification and Testing**
- Cross-customer data isolation controls shall be tested at least annually through penetration testing or equivalent verification
- Isolation failures shall be treated as security incidents subject to incident response procedures
- Verification results shall be documented and retained for audit purposes

### 6. Personal Data Leakage Prevention (AIUC-1: A006)

**6.1 PII Identification and Classification**
- Personally identifiable information (PII) shall be identified and classified within AI data pipelines
- PII handling policies shall define acceptable processing, storage, and output generation involving personal data
- Data processing agreements shall specify PII handling requirements for AI system providers

**6.2 Output Boundary Enforcement**
- User-specific output boundaries shall be enforced to prevent personal data from one user appearing in another user's outputs
- Data segregation controls shall isolate user sessions to prevent cross-user PII exposure
- Automated detection and redaction mechanisms shall be deployed to identify and remove PII from AI outputs before delivery to users

**6.3 PII Monitoring**
- Continuous monitoring shall be implemented to detect PII appearing in AI outputs where it should not be present
- PII leakage incidents shall be logged, investigated, and reported in accordance with breach notification requirements
- Regular assessments shall verify the effectiveness of PII protection controls

### 7. Third-Party IP Rights Protection (AIUC-1: A007)

**7.1 Copyright and Trademark Safeguards**
- Foundation model provider protections for copyright and trademark handling shall be documented and assessed
- Supplementary filtering shall be established to detect and prevent AI outputs that reproduce copyrighted content or infringe trademarks
- Content generation guardrails shall prevent verbatim reproduction of protected works beyond fair use thresholds

**7.2 Incident Response for IP Violations**
- Incident response procedures shall be maintained for potential intellectual property infringement in AI outputs
- Infringement reports shall be investigated, documented, and remediated within defined timeframes
- Repeat infringement patterns shall trigger enhanced filtering and model adjustment procedures

**7.3 Third-Party Data Sharing**
- Agreements governing AI-related data sharing with third parties shall include IP protection obligations
- Third-party access to AI-processed data shall be limited to defined purposes with contractual safeguards
- Data sharing agreements shall specify liability allocation for IP violations arising from AI-generated content

## Sanctions and Enforcement

**Policy Violations:**
AI data privacy violations will result in appropriate corrective action:

- **Unauthorized Data Access**: Immediate AI system access suspension and investigation
- **PII Leakage**: Incident response activation with breach notification assessment
- **Cross-Customer Exposure**: System suspension, customer notification, and root cause analysis
- **IP Rights Violations**: Content removal, infringement assessment, and remediation

**Corrective Actions:**
- Mandatory retraining on AI data handling procedures
- Enhanced monitoring and approval requirements for AI data access
- Progressive disciplinary action for repeated violations
- Regulatory reporting and customer notification as required by law
