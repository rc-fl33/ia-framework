# ISO 19011:2011 Assessor Playbook

Assessment methodology, interview guides, scoring rubrics, and document request lists for
evaluating an organization's audit programme against ISO 19011:2011 guidelines. Use this
playbook to structure audit programme quality reviews, evaluate auditor competence frameworks,
and assess internal audit programme credibility when evaluating conformance with management
system Clause 9.2 requirements.

**Related:** `iso-19011-handbook.md` (framework overview), `iso-19011-test-playbook.md` (executable test cases)

---

## 1. Assessment Overview

### Assessment Context

ISO 19011:2011 is a guidelines standard — it provides guidance for auditing management
systems and is not itself a certifiable standard. Assessors use this playbook in three
contexts:

1. **Management system Clause 9.2 evaluation:** When evaluating ISO 42001 (or other
   management system) conformance, CB auditors use ISO 19011:2011 as the benchmark for
   what a credible internal audit programme looks like. An organization claiming Clause 9.2
   conformance must demonstrate its audit programme meets the intent of ISO 19011:2011.

2. **Internal audit programme maturity review:** Organizations wanting to evaluate and
   improve their internal audit programme can use this playbook as a structured self-
   assessment against the ISO 19011:2011 framework.

3. **Second-party audit quality review:** Organizations with supplier audit programmes
   can use ISO 19011:2011 to evaluate whether their supplier audits meet the standard's
   quality framework.

### Structure of ISO 19011:2011

The standard is organized into three primary functional areas:

**Clause 5 — Managing an Audit Programme**
Covers the programme infrastructure: establishing objectives (5.2), establishing the
programme with roles, competence requirements, procedures, and records (5.3), implementing
the programme including auditor selection and scheduling (5.4), monitoring the programme
(5.5), and reviewing and improving the programme (5.6).

**Clause 6 — Performing an Audit**
Covers individual audit execution: initiating the audit including defining objectives,
scope, and criteria (6.2); conducting document review before on-site activities (6.3);
preparing the audit plan and work assignments (6.4); conducting on-site activities including
the opening meeting, evidence collection, generating findings, preparing conclusions, and
the closing meeting (6.5); preparing and distributing the audit report (6.6); completing
the audit (6.7); and conducting follow-up activities (6.8).

**Clause 7 — Competence and Evaluation of Auditors**
Covers the auditor competence framework: determining competence needs (7.2), establishing
evaluation criteria (7.3), selecting evaluation methods (7.4), conducting evaluation (7.5),
and maintaining and improving competence (7.6).

### Assessment Sequencing

1. Request audit programme documentation and records before on-site activity (see Section 2)
2. Interview: Audit Programme Manager — programme design, objectives, scheduling, monitoring
3. Interview: Audit Team Leaders — planning quality, evidence methodology, finding quality
4. Interview: Auditee Representatives — audit experience from the receiving end
5. Review: Two to three completed audit reports in detail (one recent, one from 12+ months ago)
6. Review: Corrective action follow-up records for findings from the older audit cycle
7. Review: Auditor competence framework and evaluation records

## 2. Pre-Assessment Document Request List

Request the following documents before conducting interviews. Non-receipt is an initial
gap indicator.

| Document | Clause | Red Flag If Missing |
|----------|--------|---------------------|
| Audit programme documentation (objectives, scope, procedures) | 5.2, 5.3 | No programme infrastructure; audits are ad hoc events |
| Audit programme schedule for current and prior cycle | 5.4 | Cannot assess risk-based scheduling |
| Audit programme risk identification records | 5.3.1 | Programme risks not identified or managed |
| Audit programme manager role definition | 5.3.2 | Programme responsibility not formally assigned |
| Auditor qualification records and competence framework | 5.3.3, 5.4, 7 | Auditor competence not verified or defined |
| Independence or impartiality confirmation records for recent audits | 5.4 | Auditor independence not confirmed before assignment |
| Audit programme records: plans, reports, NCRs, CA tracking | 5.3.5 | Record-keeping not established |
| Programme performance review records | 5.5, 5.6 | Programme effectiveness not evaluated |
| Audit initiation records with objectives, scope, and criteria | 6.2.2 | Audit inputs not formally defined |
| Document review records (pre-on-site) for recent audits | 6.3 | Document review not conducted before on-site activities |
| Audit plans for two to three recent audits | 6.4.1 | Planning quality cannot be assessed |
| Audit working notes or evidence collection records | 6.5.5 | Evidence methodology cannot be assessed |
| Audit reports for two to three recent audits | 6.6.1 | Finding quality and completeness cannot be assessed |
| Nonconformity and corrective action records for past cycle | 6.8 | Follow-up quality cannot be assessed |
| Auditor evaluation records | 7.5 | Auditor evaluation not conducted |
| CPD records for active auditors | 7.6 | Competence currency not maintained |

## 3. Interview Guide: Audit Programme Manager

**Target interviewees:** Audit Programme Manager, Quality Manager, Compliance Lead

**Opening question:** "Walk me through the overall audit programme — why does it exist,
what is it trying to achieve, and how is it structured?"

**Follow-up questions:**
- "Show me the documented programme objectives. How do they connect to the management
  system policy and objectives of the system being audited?"
- "Clause 5.3.1 requires identifying risks to the audit programme itself — what risks
  have you identified that could prevent the programme from achieving its objectives?
  What mitigating actions are in place?"
- "How do you decide which areas to audit, at what frequency, and with how much time
  allocated? Walk me through the risk assessment underlying the current schedule."
- "When did you last audit the AI impact assessment process? The data governance controls?
  How does the time allocation reflect the risk level of those areas versus lower-risk
  administrative processes?"
- "How do you evaluate whether the audit programme is working? What indicators do you use?
  When was the last programme review and what did it conclude?"
- "What do you report to top management about the audit programme? Show me the most recent
  management report on audit results — Clause 5.6 requires reporting programme review
  results to top management."
- "How has the audit programme changed in the past 12 months? What triggered those changes?"
- "What competence do you have to manage this audit programme? Clause 5.3.3 requires the
  programme manager to have competence appropriate to the programme scope."

**Documentation to request:**
- Audit programme document with objectives section
- Programme schedule with risk-based rationale documented
- Programme risk identification records with mitigating actions
- Programme performance review records
- Management reporting on audit programme results

**Red flags:**
- Programme objectives cannot be produced; "the programme is to conduct audits" is
  circular — Clause 5.2 requires objectives that reflect management priorities and risks
- All areas allocated identical audit time regardless of risk — flat scheduling, no
  risk-based approach per Clause 5.4
- Programme manager has no defined role or authority; the Clause 5.3.2 responsibilities
  (establish, implement, monitor, review, improve) are not formally assigned to anyone
- Programme has not changed in 3+ years despite significant expansion of the AI portfolio
  or new regulatory requirements — Clause 5.6 requires ongoing review and improvement
- Top management receives individual audit reports but no programme-level summary;
  cannot see trends across audit cycles — Clause 5.6 reporting requirement not met
- No risks to the audit programme have been identified — Clause 5.3.1 explicitly requires
  this

**Scoring guidance:**
- **Score 0:** No audit programme; individual audits conducted without programme infrastructure;
  no objectives, no schedule, no competence requirements
- **Score 1:** Schedule exists; no objectives; no risk identification; no performance monitoring;
  audit programme manager role informally held
- **Score 2:** Objectives documented; schedule partially differentiated; programme manager
  role defined; risk identification attempted but incomplete; performance review informal
- **Score 3:** Objectives documented and consistent with management system; risk-based
  schedule with documented rationale; programme risks identified with mitigating actions;
  programme performance reviewed at defined intervals with documented results; top
  management reporting on programme results
- **Score 4:** Level 3 plus: programme effectiveness measured with metrics; programme
  continuously adjusted based on risk profile changes; management actively engages with
  programme results; auditor performance trends tracked

## 4. Interview Guide: Audit Team Leaders — Planning and Execution

**Target interviewees:** Internal Audit Team Leader, Lead Auditor

**Opening question:** "Walk me through how you prepare for and conduct an audit — from
the moment an audit is assigned to you, what do you do first?"

**Follow-up questions:**
- "Before going on-site, what documents do you review? Clause 6.3 requires conducting
  a document review before on-site activities. How does what you find in the document
  review influence your audit plan?"
- "Show me the audit plan for the most recent audit you led. Walk me through how you
  built it. What are the required elements per Clause 6.4.1?"
- "How do you allocate time in the audit plan? What determines how long you spend on
  each area? Where are the areas that got more time than others, and why?"
- "What evidence collection methods do you use during an audit? Clause 6.5.5 lists
  interviews, observation, document review, record review, and data analysis. Walk me
  through the last audit — which of these methods did you actually use?"
- "When someone tells you 'we always do X,' what do you do? Clause 6.5.5 requires
  information obtained through interview to be verified through other independent sources —
  how do you corroborate claims?"
- "How do you handle a situation where an interview reveals an issue outside your planned
  audit scope? Clause 6.5.6 requires evaluating all collected information against audit
  criteria — do you follow the trail or note it out of scope?"
- "Show me a finding from a recent audit. Walk me through why you classified it as major
  or minor, and what objective evidence supports it. Clause 6.5.6 requires findings to
  be based on objective evidence evaluated against audit criteria."
- "How do you verify that a corrective action actually worked? What distinguishes
  implementation from effectiveness under Clause 6.8?"

**Documentation to request:**
- Document review records from before the most recent on-site audit
- Audit plan from the most recently completed audit (with creation date)
- Working notes or evidence collection records showing methods used
- Finding documentation (3-5 findings of mixed severity) with requirement references
  and objective evidence
- Corrective action follow-up records for findings from a prior audit cycle

**Red flags:**
- Document review described as "reading the documents during the audit" — not before it;
  violates Clause 6.3 sequence
- Audit plan is a generic checklist with no time allocation, specific scope definition,
  or criteria reference — Clause 6.4.1 required elements absent
- Evidence collection is exclusively document review; no interviews conducted — process
  execution never verified, only documentation reviewed
- "We check that the documentation exists" as the primary audit technique — corroboration
  absent; Clause 6.5.5 requires verification through independent sources
- Unexpected issues noted as "out of scope" during fieldwork and not followed — Clause
  6.5.6 requires pursuing evidence wherever it leads
- Corrective action verified by receiving an auditee confirmation email; no independent
  implementation check; effectiveness never assessed

**Scoring guidance:**
- **Score 0:** No structured audit methodology; audits are informal conversations or
  document collection exercises with no plan
- **Score 1:** Some planning; primary method is document review; findings lack requirement
  references or objective evidence; no follow-up process
- **Score 2:** Written plans with some required elements; multiple evidence methods attempted
  but inconsistently; findings have clause references but evidence descriptions are
  interpretive; follow-up occurs but effectiveness not assessed
- **Score 3:** Risk-based plans with all Clause 6.4.1 elements; multiple evidence methods
  applied systematically; interview claims corroborated; findings factual with specific
  requirement references and objective evidence; corrective action effectiveness verified
- **Score 4:** Level 3 plus: audit trails proactively followed; data analysis used
  alongside interviews and observation; corrective action trends analysed across cycles;
  opening and closing meetings structured and documented consistently

## 5. Interview Guide: Auditee Representatives — Audit Quality from the Receiving End

**Target interviewees:** AI Governance Lead, Process Owner of a recently audited process,
Department Manager

**Opening question:** "Walk me through your experience of the most recent internal audit —
what happened, and what was the outcome from your perspective?"

**Follow-up questions:**
- "How much advance notice did you receive? Was the audit plan shared with you before
  the audit started? Clause 6.4.1 requires the plan to be communicated to the auditee
  in advance."
- "What did the opening meeting cover? Clause 6.5.1 requires the opening meeting to
  cover: introduction, confirmation of objectives and scope, communication processes
  during the audit, confidentiality, and arrangements for closing meeting. Were all
  of these addressed?"
- "What happened during the audit? Who was involved from the audit team? What methods
  did they use — only documents, or also interviews and observation?"
- "Were findings reviewed with you before the closing meeting? Clause 6.5.6 requires
  the audit team to review findings with the auditee before the closing meeting."
- "Were any findings issued that you disagreed with? How was that handled? Clause 6.5.8
  requires the closing meeting to record unresolved diverging opinions."
- "What corrective actions did you implement? Did the audit team follow up to verify
  effectiveness — not just that the action was taken, but that it worked?"
- "What value did the audit provide to your area? Did it identify issues you genuinely
  needed to address, or did it feel like a compliance exercise?"

**Documentation to request:**
- Opening meeting agenda or attendance record
- Findings communicated to the auditee (for comparison with findings in the audit report)
- Corrective action submissions and audit team response records

**Red flags:**
- Auditee received audit plan the morning of the audit; advance communication requirement
  per Clause 6.4.1 not met
- Opening meeting described as "they briefly introduced themselves and started asking
  for documents" — Clause 6.5.1 required elements not covered; confidentiality obligations
  not addressed
- Auditee disagrees with findings as factually incorrect; disagreement not documented
  in audit report — Clause 6.5.8 requires unresolved diverging opinions to be recorded
- Corrective action submitted and closed with no follow-up verification; Clause 6.8
  requires the audit team leader to be responsible for verifying effectiveness
- Auditee perceives the audit as a checkbox exercise; findings identify no genuine issues —
  either the programme is superficial or the auditee area has a transparency gap

**Scoring guidance:**
- **Score 0:** Auditee unaware of any internal audit activity in recent memory
- **Score 1:** Audit occurred; minimal notice; opening meeting skipped or perfunctory;
  no corrective action follow-up
- **Score 2:** Audit structured; opening meeting conducted; findings issued; finding review
  with auditee before closing meeting not evidenced; follow-up limited to implementation
  confirmation
- **Score 3:** Appropriate advance notice; structured opening meeting covering Clause 6.5.1
  elements; findings reviewed with auditee before closing meeting; disagreements documented;
  corrective actions followed to effectiveness verification
- **Score 4:** Level 3 plus: auditee finds genuine value in audit findings; audit output
  contributes to management system improvement beyond addressing specific nonconformities

## 6. Interview Guide: Auditor Competence and Development

**Target interviewees:** Audit Programme Manager, Lead Auditor, HR/L&D where auditor
development is managed

**Opening question:** "How does your organization ensure that internal auditors have and
maintain the competence needed to conduct effective audits of your management system?"

**Follow-up questions:**
- "Clause 7.2 requires auditor competence to be determined taking into account the
  management system standard, the auditee's processes, and sector-specific knowledge.
  Show me the documented competence requirements for your AIMS auditors — what knowledge
  and skills beyond general auditing are required?"
- "Clause 7.4 lists evaluation methods: records review, feedback from referees,
  interviews, observation during auditing, role play, written examination, and post-audit
  review. Which of these methods do you use? Show me the evaluation records."
- "How often are auditors evaluated? Clause 7.5 requires evaluation — is this only at
  initial qualification or ongoing?"
- "What continuing professional development do auditors complete? Clause 7.6 requires
  maintaining and improving competence through CPD. Show me the CPD records for your
  active auditors for the past 12 months."
- "How many audits does each active auditor conduct per year? Clause 7.6 notes the
  importance of maintaining practical auditing skills through regular participation — is
  the current activity level sufficient?"
- "When the EU AI Act or an ISO standard affecting your management system scope was
  updated, how did you update auditor competence? What specifically was done?"

**Documentation to request:**
- Auditor competence framework with domain-specific requirements for the management
  system scope
- Auditor evaluation records for active auditors, showing evaluation methods used
- CPD records for active auditors (past 12 months)
- Evidence of competence framework update when the standard or regulatory context changed

**Red flags:**
- Competence requirements are "certified lead auditor" with no management system-specific
  domain requirements — Clause 7.2 explicitly requires sector and standard-specific knowledge
- Auditor evaluation conducted only at initial qualification; Clause 7.5 implies ongoing
  evaluation as part of competence maintenance
- CPD records are awareness-level training certificates; no evidence of substantive
  development on management system specifics, AI regulatory requirements, or evolving
  audit methodologies
- Auditors conducting fewer than two audits per year; practical skills eroding as
  noted in Clause 7.6 guidance on maintaining competence through regular audit activity
- No process to update competence requirements when ISO 42001 guidance evolves or EU
  AI Act obligations affect audit scope; auditors operating on outdated knowledge

**Scoring guidance:**
- **Score 0:** No competence framework; auditors self-selected without evaluation criteria;
  no CPD requirement
- **Score 1:** Initial qualification required; no domain-specific requirements; no
  evaluation methodology; no CPD tracking
- **Score 2:** Competence requirements documented but generic; initial evaluation conducted;
  CPD inconsistent; no domain-specific requirements for AIMS or sector context
- **Score 3:** Domain-specific competence requirements defined per Clause 7.2; evaluation
  uses at least two Clause 7.4 methods; CPD tracked and substantive; minimum audit
  activity maintained; competence requirements updated when standard or regulatory context
  changes
- **Score 4:** Level 3 plus: post-audit review systematically used to assess auditor
  performance; competence requirements proactively updated as management system evolves;
  sector-specific AI and regulatory knowledge maintained and verified

## 7. Control Scoring Rubric

Score each assessed area using the five-level scale. For management system Clause 9.2
certification purposes, all assessed areas must reach Level 3 at minimum — the internal
audit programme must be credible in substance, not merely documented.

| Level | Score | Name | Definition |
|-------|-------|------|-----------|
| 0 | None | Not implemented | No evidence the programme element exists in any form |
| 1 | Initial | Ad hoc | Some audit activity occurs without programme infrastructure |
| 2 | Developing | Structured but incomplete | Programme documented; execution inconsistent; follow-up weak |
| 3 | Defined | Credible programme | Programme objectives met; consistent execution; effective follow-up |
| 4 | Managed | Measured programme | Programme effectiveness measured; auditor performance evaluated |
| 5 | Optimizing | Continuously improving | Programme adapts proactively; auditor skills advancing |

### Scoring Decision Rules

**Score 0 (None):** No evidence the programme element exists. No documentation, no awareness
among interviewees. Example: no audit plan templates exist, no programme objectives
documented, audit team formation is ad hoc.

**Score 1 (Initial):** Some activity occurs but without programme infrastructure. Example:
individual audits happen but there are no defined objectives, no risk-based scheduling, no
competence requirements for auditors, and no programme-level review.

**Score 2 (Developing):** Documentation exists but with significant gaps. Example: audit
programme document exists but lacks objectives; plans exist but Clause 6.4.1 elements are
missing; findings are classified but without requirement references or objective evidence.

**Score 3 (Defined):** Programme infrastructure in place and consistently applied. Example:
documented objectives consistent with management system policy; risk-based schedule with
documented rationale; audit plans with all Clause 6.4.1 elements; findings with objective
evidence and specific requirement references; corrective actions verified to effectiveness.

**Score 4 (Managed):** Level 3 plus measurement of effectiveness. Example: programme
performance metrics tracked; auditor evaluation using multiple Clause 7.4 methods with
trend data; post-audit review systematically applied; CA trends analysed across cycles.

**Score 5 (Optimizing):** Level 4 plus proactive improvement. Example: competence
requirements updated proactively as standards evolve; programme scope and methods adapt
based on emerging management system risks; audit quality continuously demonstrably improving.

## 8. Common Findings in Audit Programme Assessments

### Clause 5 — Programme Management Findings

**Significant gaps (equivalent to major nonconformity under a management system's Clause 9.2):**
- No documented internal audit programme; individual audits conducted without a planned
  programme infrastructure — Clause 5.2 and 5.3 require a managed programme, not ad hoc audits
- Audit programme schedule does not cover all management system clauses within the
  certification cycle — complete coverage is required for Clause 9.2 conformance
- All planned audits not conducted as scheduled; significant programme gaps with no
  documented justification
- Audit programme outputs never reported to top management — Clause 5.6 requires
  programme review results to be reported; this also feeds the management review
  requirement (Clause 9.3 of the management system)
- No audit programme manager role formally defined with Clause 5.3.2 responsibilities —
  no person is accountable for the programme

**Minor gaps:**
- Risk-based scheduling not evidenced; all areas receive identical audit depth — Clause
  5.4 intent not met
- Programme performance review never conducted; programme assumed effective — Clause
  5.5 not implemented
- Audit schedule unchanged for 3+ years despite material changes — Clause 5.6 improvement
  requirement not active
- Programme risks not identified — Clause 5.3.1 requirement not addressed

---

### Clause 6 — Audit Execution Findings

**Significant gaps:**
- Audit objectives, scope, and criteria not defined before audit planning — Clause 6.2.2
  inputs missing; plan prepared without the required foundations
- Document review not conducted before on-site activities — Clause 6.3 sequence not
  followed; document review used to collect documents during the audit, not to inform it
- No structured audit plan before on-site activities — Clause 6.4.1 not implemented
- Auditors lack demonstrated competence in the management system standard being audited —
  for AIMS: ISO 42001 knowledge and AI concepts not verified
- Independence not confirmed before assignment — auditors assigned to areas they manage
  or implemented; Clause 5.4 impartiality requirement violated
- Audit reports not issued within agreed timeframe; findings stale before corrective
  actions requested

**Minor gaps:**
- Audit plans prepared but Clause 6.4.1 required elements missing (e.g., no identification
  of audit team leader, no confidentiality requirements, no reference to prior findings)
- Evidence collection limited to document review; no interviews, observation, or record
  sampling — Clause 6.5.5 methods not applied
- Opening meeting not structured; Clause 6.5.1 required elements missing
- Audit conclusions absent from report — Clause 6.6.1 requires conclusions, not just
  findings; a finding list is not an audit report
- Auditee disagreements with findings not documented — Clause 6.5.8 requires recording
  unresolved diverging opinions

---

### Clause 6.8 / Follow-Up Findings

**Significant gaps:**
- Corrective actions accepted without root cause analysis review — symptom-only fixes
  accepted; same nonconformities recur in subsequent cycles; Clause 6.8 follow-up
  responsibility not fulfilled
- No effectiveness verification; corrective actions closed when "implemented" without
  assessing whether the underlying problem was resolved — Clause 6.8 requires verification
  of effectiveness

**Minor gaps:**
- Implementation verification limited to auditee self-report with no independent check
- Timelines for corrective action not enforced; overdue items without follow-up action

---

### Clause 7 — Auditor Competence Findings

**Significant gaps:**
- No auditor competence framework — Clause 7.2 requires determining competence to
  fulfil audit programme needs; without a framework, competence cannot be confirmed
- Auditor evaluation never conducted or conducted only at initial qualification —
  Clause 7.5 requires evaluation; initial screening alone is not sufficient
- No domain-specific competence requirements for the management system scope — Clause
  7.2 requires the framework to account for the management system standard and sector
  context; generic ISO 9001 auditor competence is not sufficient for AIMS audits

**Minor gaps:**
- Evaluation uses only one method; Clause 7.4 provides a list of methods with the
  intent of multi-method evaluation for reliability
- CPD consists of awareness training only; no substantive development on management
  system specifics, regulatory changes, or emerging audit methodologies
- No process to update competence requirements when standards or regulations change
